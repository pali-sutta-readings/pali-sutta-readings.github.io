**** Papañca-khaya-sutta-vaṇṇanā

#+begin_quote
Papañcasaññāsaṅkhāpahānanti

papañcenti yattha sayaṁ uppannā, taṁ santānaṁ vitthārenti ciraṁ ṭhapentīti papañcā, kilesā.

Visesato rāgadosamohataṇhādiṭṭhimānā. Tathā hi vuttaṁ – \\
‘‘Rāgo papañco, doso papañco, moho papañco, taṇhā papañco, diṭṭhi papañco, māno papañco’’ti. –

Apica saṁkilesaṭṭho papañcaṭṭho, kacavaraṭṭho papañcaṭṭho.
#+end_quote

# "The abandoning of the reckonings of proliferative perception"

# They proliferate — that is, wherever they themselves have arisen they expand
# that continuum and make it persist for a long time — hence "proliferations"
# (papañcā), namely, the defilements. Specifically: lust, hatred, delusion,
# craving, view, and conceit. For it is said accordingly:

# "Lust is proliferation, hatred is proliferation, delusion is proliferation,
# craving is proliferation, view is proliferation, conceit is proliferation."

# Moreover, the sense of "defilement" is the sense of papañca, and the sense of
# "refuse" is the sense of papañca.

#+begin_quote
Tattha rāgapapañcassa subhasaññā nimittaṁ, dosapapañcassa āghātavatthu,
mohapapañcassa āsavā, taṇhāpapañcassa vedanā, diṭṭhipapañcassa saññā,
mānapapañcassa vitakko nimittaṁ.
#+end_quote

Herein, the sign (occasioning cause) of the proliferation of lust is the
perception of beauty; of the proliferation of hatred, a ground for resentment;
of the proliferation of delusion, the taints; of the proliferation of craving,
feeling; of the proliferation of view, perception; and of the proliferation of
conceit, discursive thought.

#+begin_quote
Tehi papañcehi sahagatā saññā papañcasaññā. Papañcasaññānaṁ saṅkhā bhāgā
koṭṭhāsā papañcasaññāsaṅkhā. Atthato saddhiṁ nimittehi taṁtaṁpapañcassa pakkhiyo
kilesagaṇo.
#+end_quote

Perceptions conjoined with those proliferations are "proliferative perceptions"
(papañcasaññā). The reckonings, parts, or divisions of those proliferative
perceptions are the "reckonings of proliferative perception." In meaning, it is
the whole class of defilements belonging to this or that proliferation, together
with their signs.

#+begin_quote
Saññāgahaṇañcettha tassa nesaṁ sādhāraṇahetubhāvena. Vuttañhetaṁ – ‘‘saññānidānā
hi papañcasaṅkhā’’ti (su. ni. 880). Tesaṁ pahānaṁ, tena tena maggena
rāgādikilesānaṁ samucchedananti attho.
#+end_quote

And perception is mentioned here because it is the cause common to them all.

# For at that time the Blessed One reviewed how, at the seat of enlightenment in
# this final existence, by the noble path he had abandoned — together with their
# residual impressions — those defilements which had been the occasion of harm to
# him throughout his own past births numbering many hundreds of thousands of
# koṭis. And he saw the continuum of beings, given over to defilement, soiled by
# lust and the rest, as hard to extricate — like a gourd filled with sour gruel,
# like a jar brimming with buttermilk, like a rag soaked in grease. Then, with joy
# and gladness arisen in him, thinking, "So dense a thicket is this round of
# defilements, cultivated from beginningless time, yet for me it is abandoned
# without remainder — ah, how well abandoned!" he uttered an inspired utterance.
# Hence it is said: "Then the Blessed One, understanding his own abandoning of the
# reckonings of proliferative perception, on that occasion uttered this inspired
# utterance."

#+begin_quote
... Tattha yassa papañcā ṭhiti ca natthī'ti:
yasmā bhagavā attānameva paraṁ viya katvā niddisati tasmā yassa aggapuggalassa \\
vuttalakkhaṇā papañcā, tehi katā saṁsāre ṭhiti ca natthi.
#+end_quote

"In whom there are no proliferations and no standing."

Since the Blessed One refers to himself as though to another, the sense is: in
whom — in that foremost of persons — there are no proliferations of the
character described, and no standing in saṁsāra produced by them.

#+begin_quote
Nettiyaṁ pana ‘‘ṭhiti nāma anusayo’’ti (netti. 27) vuttaṁ. Anusayo hi bhavapavattiyā mūlanti. \\
Satte saṁsāre papañcentīti papañcā.
#+end_quote

In the Netti, however, it is said, "'Standing' is the underlying tendency"
(Netti 27) — for the underlying tendency is the root of the continuance of
becoming. They cause beings to be protracted in saṁsāra: hence "proliferations."
